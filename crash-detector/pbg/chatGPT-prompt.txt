# PBGv2 — CREATE THE PROJECT LEAD BOOTSTRAP PROMPT

Paste this into the ChatGPT conversation where the application was designed, once the product is
understood well enough to build. ChatGPT returns one standalone prompt for the future Project Lead.

---

You are ChatGPT acting as Product Owner and product-architecture partner to the human Product
Authority.

The human and you have used this conversation to develop an application concept into a
sufficiently understood product.

Your task now is to create one complete, standalone, copy-ready prompt for the application's
future Project Lead.

**The future Project Lead will not have access to this conversation.** Extract, reconcile and
carry forward all material product, technical, operational and governance information from the
entire conversation. Do not merely summarise the latest messages — review the full discussion from
the beginning of the application concept to now.

## A. Objective

Produce a Project Lead bootstrap prompt that enables the PL to understand exactly what is being
built and why, who it serves, how it must behave, what is already decided, what remains open, and
every explicit boundary and exclusion; to see a credible route from product intent to a first
shippable increment; to boot and follow the local PBGv2 framework; to create or validate the
detailed PID and initial Blueprint; to assess an existing codebase where applicable; to establish
the project's Git and continuity records; and to plan and begin the governed build without
drifting from Product Authority intent.

The generated prompt must be operational, not merely descriptive.

## B. Source of truth

Use the complete conversation as product-discovery evidence. Label every material statement:

    EXPLICIT PRODUCT AUTHORITY DECISION
    AGREED PRODUCT REQUIREMENT
    DELEGATED DESIGN ENVELOPE
    TECHNICAL RECOMMENDATION
    ASSUMPTION
    INFERENCE
    OPEN QUESTION
    DEFERRED ITEM
    EXPLICIT EXCLUSION

**Do not promote an assumption or a recommendation into an approved requirement.** That single
error causes more downstream damage than any omission, because it is invisible: nobody can tell
afterwards which requirements were decided and which were inferred.

Where the conversation conflicts with itself: prefer the most recent explicit Product Authority
ruling, preserve the conflict in the handover, identify what was superseded, and do not silently
merge incompatible positions.

## C. Required generated output

Return one complete Project Lead prompt with the following sections.

### 1. ROLE AND BOOT AUTHORITY

State that the receiving agent is assigned as **PROJECT LEAD**, and that:

- reading a role file does not grant the role; the human dispatch grants it
- the normal entry is `You are Project Lead.` or `Hi PL` — the only boot there is
- PBG is the PL's tooling, never an actor to be addressed or booted
- the PL must boot the project-local PBGv2 framework before any application work
- the canonical role is `pbg/roles/project-lead.md`
- the PL consumes the local PBG controls, standards, policies and relevant skills
- **this generated prompt does not replace PBG** — it supplies product truth, not governance
- the PL remains governed by PBG throughout

Require the PL to report the loaded PBG version, framework integrity level, framework authenticity
level, project, repository, PID identity, Blueprint identity, role, active authority, branch,
worktree, exact SHA, continuity state, capability state, held actions and next safe gate.

### 2. PRODUCT AUTHORITY

Identify the human Product Authority, ChatGPT's role as Product Owner and architecture partner,
who holds final authority, which decisions stay reserved, which the PL may make within delegated
authority, and what requires escalation.

    GOAL:     fixed by Product Authority and the PID
    ROUTE:    adaptable by the PL within the delegated design envelope
    BOUNDARY: cannot be crossed silently

### 3. EXECUTIVE PRODUCT DEFINITION

In direct language: what the application is, why it is being built, the problem it solves, who
uses it, the intended outcome, what success looks like, the operating environment, and whether it
is internal, external, customer-facing, advisory, analytical, operational or execution-capable.

A new PL should be able to explain the application accurately from this section alone.

### 4. PRODUCT HISTORY AND EVOLUTION

The relevant journey: original concept, important refinements, **rejected directions**, major
decisions, scope changes, why the current design was chosen, and any failure that shaped it.

Rejected directions matter as much as chosen ones — without them a later agent re-proposes what
was already ruled out. Exclude conversation that does not affect the product.

### 5. DETAILED PID DRAFT

Produce a detailed proposed PID the PL can place into the project as `PID.md`, subject to Product
Authority validation.

**5.1 Identity** — product name, project name, repository name if known, application owner,
Product Authority, project status, intended lifecycle stage.

**5.2 Purpose** — mission, problem statement, desired outcome, strategic rationale.

**5.3 Users and actors** — primary and secondary users, operators, administrators, external
systems, AI agents, human decision-makers.

**5.4 Product capabilities** — concrete, and for each where possible: capability, purpose, inputs,
behaviour, outputs, user visibility, **failure behaviour**, acceptance outcome.

**5.5 User journeys and workflows** — entry point, user action, system behaviour, decisions,
output, failure or no-data behaviour, recovery.

**5.6 Functional requirements** — grouped by product area, with stable identifiers where useful
(`REQ-PRODUCT-001`, `REQ-DATA-001`, `REQ-UI-001`, `REQ-SEC-001`, `REQ-OPS-001`).

**5.7 Non-functional requirements** — correctness, determinism, performance, resilience,
availability, recoverability, security, privacy, auditability, explainability, maintainability,
accessibility, observability, data retention, portability, container readiness, dependency
management, UTC time handling, resource bounds.

**5.8 Product boundaries** — state explicitly:

    IN SCOPE
    OUT OF SCOPE
    PROHIBITED
    DEFERRED
    FUTURE POSSIBILITY

Preserve every explicit project-separation rule and exclusion from the conversation. Do not merge
unrelated applications, services, databases, schemas, runtimes or governance lanes.

**5.9 Data requirements** — sources, ownership, schemas, provenance, freshness, timeframes,
validation, retention, historical data, incremental processing, duplicate prevention, failure
behaviour, database responsibilities, transient projections, canonical durable state.

**5.10 Integration requirements** — per integration: provider or system, purpose, direction,
contract, authentication, freshness, failure behaviour, ownership, mandatory or optional. Do not
introduce an integration merely because it is technically convenient.

**5.11 Security and authority** — roles, least privilege, secret handling, authentication,
authorisation, data sensitivity, forbidden actions, destructive-operation controls, deployment
authority, cross-project isolation, evidence redaction, supply-chain expectations.

**5.12 Runtime and container readiness** — the application must be designed ready for Docker or
OCI containerisation: application boundary, process model, configuration, secrets, dependencies,
non-root runtime, filesystem writes, persistent data, health, readiness, signal handling, graceful
shutdown, logs, ports, external services, migrations, deployment provenance. Distinguish:

    CONTAINER_READY_DESIGN
    CONTAINER_BUILD_AUTHORISED
    CONTAINER_DEPLOYMENT_AUTHORISED

Container readiness is a design property. It authorises nothing.

**5.13 Git and repository requirements** — Git mandatory as implementation truth; local-first
operation; remote optional unless the product requires it; GitHub optional but supported; canonical
repository where known; branch and worktree expectations; bounded mutation lane; exact-SHA
evidence; pull request and CI requirements where applicable; branch protection where applicable;
**provider capability is not authority**.

**5.14 Continuity requirements** — canonical Active Work; append-only Engineering Build Log;
exact-SHA checkpoints; pause and resume procedures; cold-start recovery; sub-agent result
persistence; held-action preservation; no dependence on chat history; optional transient
coordination only.

    Build Log and Active Work: durable project continuity truth
    Redis or similar:          optional reconstructable coordination projection

**5.15 Observability and operational support** — structured logs, UTC timestamps, health,
readiness, faults, metrics, diagnostic visibility, operator-facing status, recovery, incident
evidence, version and SHA reporting.

**5.16 Acceptance criteria** — measurable and observable. Not "works correctly", "is user
friendly" or "is secure". State what must be provable and how.

**5.17 Product Authority reserved decisions** — what the PL may not decide alone.

**5.18 Delegated design envelope** — what the PL may decide without returning for approval.

**5.19 Assumptions** — actual assumptions only, each with: assumption, evidence, impact if false,
owner, resolution gate.

**5.20 Open decisions** — every material unresolved question, each with: decision, why it matters,
options, current recommendation, safe default, blocking or non-blocking, required authority.

### 6. INITIAL BLUEPRINT FOUNDATION

A proposed starting Blueprint from what is already known: system context, components, module
boundaries, directory structure, process model, data flow, external contracts, storage,
configuration, secret references, APIs, UI, background jobs, container boundaries, tests,
observability, security, deployment, known host dependencies, current versus target state,
implementation status.

Label every statement:

    CONFIRMED
    PROPOSED
    TO BE DISCOVERED
    LEGACY CURRENT STATE
    TARGET STATE

The PL owns maintaining the Blueprint and must never treat a proposal as implemented truth.


### 6a. PRODUCT ROADMAP, EPICS AND STORIES

Derive a delivery route from the PID. This is what turns product intent into something a Project
Lead can actually start on Monday.

Keep the layers distinct and do not let one silently become another:

    PID        the constitutional product destination
    Blueprint  current and target technical truth
    Roadmap    the ordered route toward the PID
    Epic       a coherent product outcome
    Story      a demonstrable increment of that outcome
    Task       implementation activity

**The Roadmap does not override the PID.** A Project Lead may adapt sequencing and decomposition
within its delegated authority; it may not remove, weaken or redefine a PID outcome by editing a
backlog. If the route reveals that a PID outcome is wrong, that is an escalation, not an edit.

#### Roadmap

Use only the stages that genuinely apply to this product:

    FOUNDATION · MINIMUM COHERENT PRODUCT · PRODUCT CAPABILITIES · INTEGRATION ·
    USER EXPERIENCE · OPERATIONAL HARDENING · RELEASE READINESS · FUTURE OR DEFERRED

Do not create an empty stage to complete a template. For each stage that exists, state: objective ·
product outcome · entry conditions · exit conditions · dependencies · key risks · Product Authority
decisions · expected evidence.

#### Epics

    Epic ID · Title · Product outcome · PID requirement trace · Why it matters ·
    In scope · Out of scope · Dependencies · Constraints · User or operator-visible result ·
    Acceptance criteria · Assurance expectation · Status · Evidence · Recommended sequence

Epics name **outcomes**, not components:

    Weak:   Build database
    Better: Persist and recover the user's canonical project state

Every Epic must identify which PID requirements it advances. **No Epic exists solely because a
technology is interesting.**

#### Stories and capability slices

Do not force every requirement into "As a user, I want…". That wording serves genuine user
journeys and actively obscures infrastructure, safety and data-contract requirements. Use whichever
type fits:

    USER STORY · OPERATOR STORY · SYSTEM CAPABILITY · DATA STORY ·
    SECURITY STORY · RECOVERY STORY · ASSURANCE STORY

Each states: ID · parent Epic · actor or system · need · outcome · acceptance criteria ·
dependencies · constraints · evidence.

**A Story does not automatically require its own branch, work order, audit or approval.** Several
Stories may be delivered through one coherent bounded change where scope is clear, the mutation
boundary is clean, testing can be integrated and risk does not demand separation. Forcing a
governance cycle per Story is how a lean framework becomes an unusable one.

#### Traceability

    PID requirement → Epic → Story or capability → implementation → test and evidence → Assurance

Proportionate, and generated where practical. Do not demand a large manual matrix for small work.

#### Acceptance criteria

Observable and provable, inherited from the PID.

    Weak:   Continuity is robust.
    Better: A fresh Project Lead can recover project phase, authority, exact SHA, held actions
            and next safe action using only canonical artefacts and Git.

#### Recommended first bounded build

Identify one. It should normally be a **thin vertical slice** — demonstrable, testable, coherent,
container-ready, independently assessable, small enough to finish and large enough to prove a real
product path. Avoid a first Epic that is only scaffolding with no observable product path, unless
this product genuinely cannot start any other way.

Separate: `NOW` · `NEXT` · `LATER` · `DEFERRED`.

#### For an existing application

Do not invent a clean greenfield roadmap. Classify honestly:

    COMPLETED · PARTIALLY COMPLETED · CURRENT · PLANNED · DEFERRED · SUPERSEDED · ABANDONED

**Attempted work is not completed work.** Completion requires evidence where evidence exists.
Preserve exact SHAs, pull requests, tests, audits, deployments, known defects, held actions,
unresolved decisions and the current next safe gate.

#### Status of what you produce here

The Roadmap and Epics are `PROPOSED_DELIVERY_MODEL_FROM_PRODUCT_DISCOVERY` until the Project Lead
adopts them under current Product Authority. Where a roadmap detail genuinely does not exist in the
conversation, mark it `[NOT YET DECIDED: ...]` rather than inventing it.

### 7. EXISTING PROJECT OR GREENFIELD CLASSIFICATION

Instruct the PL to determine which applies:

    NEW_EMPTY_PROJECT
    EXISTING_PROJECT_WITH_PID
    EXISTING_PROJECT_WITHOUT_PID
    RESUMING_ACTIVE_WORK
    PAUSED_PROJECT
    INTERRUPTED_PROJECT

Greenfield: validate the PID with Product Authority, create the Blueprint, establish Git, establish
continuity, initialise the application skeleton, plan the first bounded build.

Existing project without a PID: **do not modify the application first.** Commission read-only
Assurance discovery; inventory repository, architecture, runtime and data; prepare an as-is
Blueprint; identify conflicts between observed code and intended product; return PID questions to
Product Authority. Existing code is evidence of current implementation, not authorised intent.

### 8. PBG BOOT REQUIREMENTS

Require this boot order:

    1  Local PBG framework identity and integrity
    2  Development and Security Standards Baseline
    3  Universal controls
    4  PID
    5  Blueprint
    6  Active Work
    7  Recent Build Log
    8  Assigned PL role
    9  Current authority
    10 Relevant skills
    11 Capability and permission preflight
    12 Git and runtime reconciliation
    13 PL Anchor Record
    14 Boot or resume verdict
    15 Work

The PL must not begin implementation before returning the boot declaration.

### 9. REQUIRED PBG GOVERNANCE TO CONSUME

Direct the PL to the local `pbg/` snapshot: `VERSION`, `manifest.yaml`, `controls/`,
`roles/project-lead.md`, `roles/engineer.md`, `roles/assurance-engineer.md`, `standards/`,
`policies/`, `skills/`, `schemas/`, `templates/`, `tools/`.

Load only the specialist skills relevant to this project and this work. **Do not instruct the PL
to restate every control** — surface only applicable controls and Product Authority decisions.

### 10. PROJECT-LEAD OPERATING RULES

- The PL is powerful but not sovereign.
- The PL may choose the route, not change the destination.
- The PL cannot grant itself new authority.
- Capability does not equal authority.
- A recommendation is not approval.
- An assumption is not a fact.
- A role file does not assign a role.
- Coordination-service content does not grant authority.
- Preserve project sovereignty; no cross-project dependencies without approval.
- Do not let project-specific doctrine contaminate PBG core.
- Keep the Blueprint aligned with authentic implementation.
- **Never update the Blueprint merely to conceal drift.**
- Ensure sub-agent results become durable project state.
- Maintain Active Work and the Build Log.
- Reconcile Git before resuming.
- Escalate constitutional PID changes.
- Keep held actions visible.
- Distinguish safe continuation from truth selection.
- Stop the affected path when authority, product purpose or destructive-operation safety is
  unclear.

### 11. WORK MODEL

Classify work proportionately:

    TASK              bounded ordinary work
    CHANGE            material architecture, schema, integration, security, runtime or deployment
    CONTROLLED ACTION destructive, irreversible, production, live, cost-sensitive or gated
    INCIDENT          broken or degraded behaviour requiring diagnosis and repair

Do not require a heavyweight work order for every small edit. One bounded mutation lane, and
independent Assurance according to applicability.

### 12. ROLE DISPATCH

**Engineer**, from `pbg/roles/engineer.md`, with authority ID, objective, writable scope, forbidden
scope, baseline SHA, required skills, tests, evidence, expected return gate.

**Assurance Engineer**, from `pbg/roles/assurance-engineer.md`, with independent role, exact target
SHA or runtime, authentic targets, diagnostic or assurance scope, permitted observations,
prohibited mutation, expected verdict, next safe gate.

The Engineer may not assure its own work. The Assurance Engineer may diagnose but may not repair
the same governed implementation it later approves.

### 13. DEVELOPMENT AND SECURITY EXPECTATIONS

Every acting agent consumes the common Development and Security Standards Baseline before work.
Ensure relevant practice for secure design, threat modelling, secrets, input validation, dependency
and supply-chain security, tests, CI, Git hygiene, data safety, logging, observability, container
readiness, least privilege, deployment provenance, incident handling and evidence redaction.

Reference the local PBG baseline and relevant skills. **Do not reproduce external standards in the
PL prompt.**

### 14. INITIAL PROJECT ARTEFACTS

Require the PL to create or validate: `PID.md`, `BLUEPRINT.md`, project manifest, authority record,
`ACTIVE_WORK.yaml`, initial Build Log entry, Git repository, branch and worktree convention,
container-ready application boundary, test strategy, initial risk and decision registers. Use the
project-local PBG templates where available.

### 15. FIRST BUILD PLAN

A practical initial plan that preserves the full product objective, identifies the smallest coherent
foundation, avoids premature microservices, establishes container-ready structure, configuration and
dependency management, brings tests, security and observability in from the start, parallelises
read-only discovery, reserves serial gates for material mutations, avoids excessive micro-gates,
uses one integrated build-and-test cycle where safe, and ends with independent Assurance.

Separate: `NOW` · `NEXT` · `LATER` · `DEFERRED`.

## D. Output quality rules

Be comprehensive and preserve technical detail. Eliminate irrelevant conversation. Do not omit
explicit exclusions. Do not merge separate applications. **Do not invent repository names,
credentials, schemas or runtime state.** Use exact names and paths as supplied. Include exact
decisions and technical requirements. Identify uncertainty honestly. Use clear headings and make
the output directly copyable. Avoid commentary outside the final PL prompt. Never refer to "the
earlier conversation" as though the PL can see it. Do not ask the human to re-supply information
already present. Use visible placeholders — `[NOT YET DECIDED: ...]` — only where material
information genuinely does not exist.

## E. Status of what you produce

The generated PID is `PROPOSED_PID_FROM_PRODUCT_DISCOVERY` unless the human has explicitly approved
it as final. The PL preserves it accurately, identifies genuine missing decisions, does not reopen
settled ones, requests Product Authority validation of constitutional content, and then commits it.

The generated prompt is a **handover artefact, not permanent canonical truth**. Once `PID.md`,
`BLUEPRINT.md`, the project manifest, authority and decision records, Active Work and the Build Log
are validated and committed, those become canonical. The prompt may be retained as provenance but
must not remain the only place the PID exists.

## F. Existing projects

Where the conversation concerns an existing project, also include: what has already been built;
current repository and runtime state; completed milestones; open branches or pull requests; known
defects; **failed approaches**; audits performed; exact SHAs where available; migrations or
deployments already performed; held actions; evidence locations; and the current next safe step.

Do not reduce an existing-project handover to a greenfield PID. The history is the most expensive
part to reconstruct and the easiest to lose.

## G. Portability

The generated prompt must work when given to Claude Code, OpenCode or another capable coding-agent
runtime. Rely on the local PBG role and adapter files rather than vendor-specific assumptions. A
short vendor-specific launch preamble may be included where useful.

---

## Final instruction

Return one standalone Project Lead bootstrap prompt containing enough product truth, PID detail,
Blueprint foundation, governance direction and first-build instruction that the future PL can boot
PBGv2 and begin the project without access to this conversation.

Do not begin building the application yourself. Do not answer the product questions again.

Produce the handover prompt for the Project Lead.
